Who I am
Strategy Meets Storytelling.

Why I do this

Behind every click is a human.

The best marketing starts with understanding what people care about and building everything else around that truth.

Trends change every quarter. Platforms rise and fall. What does not change is why a person stops scrolling, why they trust one brand over another, why they share something instead of just watching it. That is the layer I actually work in. The channel is just where it shows up.

So I do not start a brief by asking what we should post. I start by asking what this audience needs to hear, and what truth the brand has been avoiding saying out loud. Everything downstream, the calendar, the copy, the campaign, exists to serve that answer.

In practice

How I usually think through a marketing problem.

I don't start with content ideas. I start by understanding what people need to hear, what the business needs to achieve, and where those two things meet.

Understand the business, audience, goals, constraints and history before suggesting solutions. Most bad strategy comes from skipping this step.

Look for the gap between what the brand wants to say and what customers actually care about. That gap is usually where the opportunity lives.

Translate insights into campaigns, content pillars, partnerships and growth opportunities. Not a moodboard, an actual operating system for the brand.

Create content, messaging and touchpoints that feel consistent across every channel, so the brand sounds like one person, not five departments.

Watch performance, engagement patterns and audience behaviour beyond vanity metrics. Reach is easy to fake. Behaviour change is not.

The best marketing systems evolve continuously. Every result creates the next hypothesis. Nothing ships and stays untouched.

Original concepts

Mock campaigns.

Three original strategic concepts showing how I think about brands, emotion and message.

Fevicol

"Kuch cheezein toot-ti nahi. Sirf bikhar-ti hain."

A stop-motion series exploring emotional permanence over product strength. The idea: some things do not break. They just scatter. A meditation on attachment, memory and what holds us together. Leads with emotion before it ever mentions a product.

Format: Stop-motion series, social-first · Tone: Quiet, philosophical
F
Nykaa

"Not ready yet. And that's the best part."

Unfinished moments. Honest process over transformation reveals. A campaign celebrating the in-between: the half-done look, the learning curve, the becoming. Refreshingly anti-highlight-reel in a category built on perfection. Shows the brand on the side of real people.

Format: Reel series, UGC-driven · Tone: Honest, warm, anti-aspirational
N
Just Herbs

"Your skin, but calmer."

Slow-cinema aesthetics. Quiet restoration over dramatic transformation. No before-and-after. Just stillness, light and skin that breathes. For a brand that earns trust through patience, not promises. Every frame is deliberately unhurried. The restraint is the message.

Format: Long-form video, ambient social content · Tone: Still, confident, sensory

The journey

How I got here.

Websites. Blogs. Product descriptions. Brand copy. Writing everything from scratch, learning that words are not decoration. They are the brand experience itself. This is where the craft started and the obsession with language and audience took hold.

Every word was a lesson ✦

Understanding how people receive and process information in real time. What makes them lean in. What makes them switch off. It made me a sharper communicator than any textbook could. Content writing started evolving into something closer to strategy.

People first, always ✦

A purely organic content strategy reached 800,000 people in a single month. Zero paid budget. Articles, social posts, digital content working together across channels. Also supported operations for HUL, ITC, BYJU'S and Native Milk. This was the proof of concept for strategic content at scale.
